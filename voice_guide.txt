MEERA PILLAI / SKINSTINCT — VOICE GUIDE
Built from 15 published pieces (4 LinkedIn posts, 11 newsletters)
=========================================================

1. OPENING PATTERNS (use one of these two — nothing else)

  A) COLD DATA/SCENE DROP
     Start with a specific number, date, or remembered moment. No throat-clearing.
     Templates:
       "In [year] I was [doing specific thing]..."
       "In the [X months] to [date], [N]% of [metric] came from [cause]."
     Examples from corpus:
       "In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting..."
       "In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns..."

  B) META-ANNOUNCEMENT
     Tell the reader what you're about to do and why, before doing it.
     Template: "I want to [explain/talk about/be honest about] X, because [reason
     this is usually done badly / skipped / misunderstood]."
     Examples:
       "I want to talk about how actives work, because I think most explainer
       content on this topic stops exactly one step before the part that matters."
       "I've been avoiding writing this one because Vitamin C is genuinely
       complicated..."

  NEVER: rhetorical questions to the reader, hype adjectives, flattery,
  product mentions in the first line.

2. SENTENCE RHYTHM

  Pattern: one or two long, subordinate-clause-heavy sentences carrying the
  technical/causal explanation, immediately followed by 2-3 short flat
  declaratives with NO connective words (no "and then," "so," "therefore").
  The short sentences land the point; the long ones earn it.

  Example (Post 2):
    "Nobody else in the room asked about it. The formulation passed review.
    I left the company 7 months later."

  Rule of thumb: if a paragraph runs past 3 lines of explanation, cut to a
  clipped 5-10 word sentence before moving on.

3. HOW DATA IS USED

  - Every number is immediately tied to a mechanism. Never state a stat and
    move on — explain what causes it or what it causes, in the same breath.
  - Cite a named source when one exists (institution, year, study type).
  - State absence of data as precisely as presence of it: "I don't know how
    to separate X from Y in that number" is a valid, on-voice sentence.
  - Prefer precise units over vague intensifiers: "pH 3.2," "2mg/cm2,"
    "4-6 months" — not "quite low," "a lot," "a while."

4. THE CLARIFYING HEDGE (use often, it's her signature move)

  Template: "I'm not saying [strawman/overclaim]. I'm saying [narrower,
  defensible claim]."
  Also: "I want to be careful/honest/precise about X" as a lead-in before
  a claim that could be misread as an attack or an overclaim.

5. STRUCTURAL DEVICES

  - Rule of three: mechanism explanations are broken into exactly three
    conditions/causes ("has to reach the site, at sufficient concentration,
    in a form that's still intact").
  - Signpost before the takeaway: "Here's what I want you to take from this."
  - Closes on an action for the READER to take toward THIRD PARTIES
    (ask any brand for documentation), not a CTA toward her own product.

6. WHAT NEVER APPEARS

  - Superlatives about her own product ("best," "revolutionary," "powerful")
  - Urgency or scarcity language
  - Exclamation points, emoji
  - Naming competitors
  - Unqualified use of "natural" / "clean" — these words only appear to be
    interrogated, never deployed approvingly
  - A hard sell or purchase CTA at the end of a newsletter

7. PERSONAL DISCLOSURE — CALIBRATION

  Disclosure is instrumental, never confessional. She shares:
    - career history (2 years in pharma, why she left)
    - specific failures (14 months failing to formulate a Vitamin C product,
      the humidity/return-rate miss)
    - moments of doubt, always resolved into a business or scientific lesson
  She does NOT share:
    - family, health, relationships, anything outside the company
  Self-deprecation is bounded and dry ("embarrassingly long," "not a
  particularly inspiring story") — never raw or self-pitying.

8. SIGN-OFF

  Always just "Meera" — no title, no company name appended.

9. QUICK CHECKLIST BEFORE PUBLISHING IN THIS VOICE

  [ ] Opens with data/scene OR meta-announcement — not both, not neither
  [ ] At least one long explanatory sentence followed by a short flat one
  [ ] Every number has a stated mechanism or consequence attached
  [ ] No superlatives, urgency, or competitor call-outs
  [ ] If disclosing something personal, it resolves into a business/science
      lesson within the same paragraph
  [ ] Ends on an action for the reader aimed outward, not a CTA
  [ ] Signed "Meera"

NOTE ON SCOPE: this guide is fit to one person's 15 samples. It captures her
tics reliably; treat it as a lens on Meera Pillai specifically, not a
general "skincare founder" voice template.
